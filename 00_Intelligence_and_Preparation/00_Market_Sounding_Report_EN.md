# Market Sounding Report – Core Banking Systems (Saudi Market)

**Prepared by:** Strategic Procurement Team  
**Date:** July 10, 2026  
**Classification:** Confidential (Internal Use Only before RFP Issuance)

---

## 1. Executive Summary (Pricing Calibration)

Previous feasibility studies estimating the project at $20M-$35M are **unrealistic** in the context of the Saudi and Gulf market for 2026. Replacing a Core Banking system for a bank the size of AlVirtu Bank requires an estimated budget ranging between **($50M – $120M USD)** over 5 years. This is attributed to:
- Costs of locally licensed cloud providers (who charge a premium for localization).
- Costs of System Integrator consulting firms (e.g., Accenture, PwC, Wipro).
- Complex Sharia-compliant product customization requirements.
- Costs of integration with national infrastructure (SADAD, SARIE, SWIFT), which often require expensive local experts.

---

## 2. Analysis of Major Vendors (The Old Guard)

**Vendor (A) – Traditional Leader:**
- **Market Share:** Very high in major regional banks.
- **Estimated Cost:** $55M – $85M.
- **Pricing Model:** Base License + Additional Modules (Islamic, Wealth).
- **Reputation:** Technically strong, notorious for complex contracts and extra charges for additional modules.
- **Legal Risks:** Has faced lawsuits for forcing mandatory upgrades requiring new hardware, increasing estimated costs by 30%.
- **Assessment:** Technically strong, but the most contractually complex.
- **Strategic Trap:** Prone to the "Unbundling Trap." Must force (All-Inclusive Licensing) covering all current and future features.

**Vendor (B) – Flexible Moderate:**
- **Market Share:** Very strong in medium and large banks.
- **Estimated Cost:** $50M – $95M.
- **Pricing Model:** Reasonable base license; SI costs separated.
- **Reputation:** Excellent flexibility, but requires strict governance over System Integrator (SI) costs.
- **Legal Risks:** Faced a class-action lawsuit in 2023 regarding misleading investors about maintenance revenue growth.
- **Assessment:** Excellent flexibility, but requires strict governance over SI costs.
- **Strategic Trap:** Requires (Joint & Several Liability) to make the primary vendor the single point of accountability for the entire project.

**Vendor (C) – Economical Aggressor:**
- **Market Share:** Strong competitor, but local presence is less dense.
- **Estimated Cost:** $45M – $75M.
- **Pricing Model:** Very attractive base license price.
- **Reputation:** Tempting licensing price, but very expensive customization (Customization Ransom).
- **Legal Risks:** Multiple acquisitions leading to instability in supporting older versions, pressuring clients to upgrade prematurely.
- **Assessment:** Tempting entry price, but it turns into a financial trap during customization.
- **Strategic Trap:** Apply (All-Inclusive Licensing) to prevent Unbundling.

---

## 3. Analysis of Modern Players (Cloud-Native Disruptors)

**Vendor (D) – Cloud-Native / Microservices (Exploratory):**
- **Advantage:** Built entirely on microservices and cloud-native architecture. High speed and flexibility.
- **Estimated Cost:** $40M – $65M.
- **Challenge:** Scarcity of local experts, weak Arabic documentation, which may increase reliance on foreign consultants.
- **Recommendation:** Include as a Benchmark option in the RFP to measure the pricing and technological gap, without commitment.

---

## 4. Analysis of Additional Hidden Costs

A. **National Systems Integration (SADAD, SARIE, SWIFT):** $5M – $10M additional.
B. **Sharia-Customizations:** Can reach 15-20% of the license value.
C. **Training & Knowledge Transfer:** Minimum $3M.
D. **Exit Strategy (Data Migration to new vendor):** Up to $10M.

---

## 5. Lessons Learned from Previous Projects (Common Mistakes)

A. Underestimating integration costs with legacy systems: 40% of cost overruns occur here.
B. Vague scope definition for the SI partner: leads to unlimited Change Orders.
C. Overlooking training and knowledge transfer costs for local staff: can reach up to 20% of the contract value.
D. Ignoring the "Vendor Exit Cost" at the end of the contract.

---

## 6. Final Recommendations for the Procurement Team

1.  Adopt an estimated budget of no less than **$75M**, with a 20% contingency fund for cost overruns.
2.  Mandate that bidders submit **two offers**: a classical implementation offer, and an experimental offer using Cloud-Native technology to measure the pricing and flexibility gap.
3.  Engage an external consultant specialized in Core Banking contracts to review the financial offers before signing, to avoid falling into the trap of a low-cost offer that ends with massive Change Order claims.

## 7. The Critical Tender Clause (Knock-out Criterion – to be inserted in RFP)

> **"Geographic Sovereignty & Compliance Clause (Knock-out Criterion):**  
> The Bidder irrevocably commits that all data (including client data, transactions, audit logs, and backups) for ALL environments (Production, UAT, Dev, Testing, DR) will be stored, processed, and managed exclusively within the geographical borders of the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, via locally licensed cloud providers. Any explicit or implicit indication in the technical or financial proposal suggesting data transfer outside the Kingdom, or reliance on non-compliant subcontractors, will result in **immediate disqualification of the entire bid without technical or financial evaluation**, and the Bidder bears all legal consequences."
