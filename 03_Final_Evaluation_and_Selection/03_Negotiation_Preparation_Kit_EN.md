## 1. Negotiation BATNA (Best Alternative to a Negotiated Agreement)

- **Bank's BATNA:** Reverting to Vendor (A) after forcing them to submit a revised bid that fully complies with Data Residency and includes All-Inclusive Licensing. (This is an expensive and weak alternative, but available).
- **Vendor (B)'s BATNA:** They may attempt to hold firm on their prices, threatening to walk away. However, the regional banking market is strategically critical for them, so losing this deal would be costly for them as well.

## 3. Bank's Negotiation Leverage Points (Power Cards)

1.  **National Sovereignty:** We can use the "Data Residency" file as a pressure card (We are the only bank in the region that made this a strict requirement, and you met it – giving you an edge, but we could open the door to new competitors if you don't meet our economic terms).
2.  **Deal Size:** The contract value exceeds $50 million, a massive deal warranting pricing concessions.
3.  **Timeline Flexibility:** We can extend or compress the implementation timeline, giving us flexibility in the financial package.
4.  **Future References:** We can commit to being a (Reference Case) for them in the region, opening doors to other banks (in exchange for additional discounts).

## 5. Red Lines (Absolute Non-Negotiables)

> **Warning:** Concession on these items is strictly prohibited. If the vendor insists, negotiations must be halted immediately:

1.  **Data Residency:** No environment (even Dev/Test) may be located outside the Kingdom. ✅ (Achieved, but must be re-confirmed in the contract).
2.  **Fixed Man-Day Rate:** Locked for the next 5 years, with no surprise extra charges.
3.  **Prevention of Unjustified Change Orders:** Any scope change must be pre-approved by the bank with a cost estimate; the vendor cannot impose unilateral changes.
4.  **Cash Penalties for SLA:** Immediate and effective financial penalties (not virtual credits) for performance drops below 99.999%.
