## 1. Executive summary

| Metric | Result |

| Three-year ledger | **USD 72.11 million** |
| Average annual spend | **USD 24.04 million** |
| Largest category | Core Systems — **63.4%** |
| Largest supplier | Vendor A — **63.4%** |
| Top two suppliers | Vendor A + Vendor B — **81.1%** |

Spend analysis is the first expert step because an RFP written without a ledger guesses at the category. This ledger shows the replacement is not a technology preference. It is the only lever that resets a 22% maintenance book, a parallel sandbox, and single-vendor concentration at the same time.

## 4. Benchmark, not just a lower price

| Test | Finding |
| :--- | :--- |
| Maintenance rate | FY2025 maintenance is 22.3% of that year’s core licence. Market-typical CBS support in this case sits at 17–18%. |
| Cloud / sandbox | USD 1.48m over three years for environments that a replacement contract can include at zero extra fee. |
| Consulting | USD 12.70m to keep an ageing core current — a running cost of delay, not a transformation benefit. |
| National rails | SADAD / SARIE / SWIFT / IPS keep rising under mandate. They are not optional savings lines. |

A 5-point cut on a USD 13.0m licence is about **USD 0.59m a year**. The larger prize is stopping the parallel opex the TCO model later prices at award.

---

## 5. Decision for the CBS business case

1. The category that must carry the investment case is Core Systems, not “IT” in general.
2. Vendor A is both the spend peak and the operational lock-in.
3. Price-only RFPs will miss the 22% maintenance ratchet and the sandbox line.
4. Savings that survive signature are rate, scope, and inclusion — not a lower first-year licence.
